Emergency information

In an emergency, act quickly.

This page provides general safety guidance. It does not replace emergency services, a clinician or the instructions of your local emergency dispatcher.

Do not wait for a website response.

For severe or life-threatening symptoms, contact your local emergency service or go to the nearest emergency department.

Recognise urgent symptoms

When to seek immediate help

These symptoms can indicate a medical emergency.

Chest pain or severe breathing difficulty

New or severe chest pressure, difficulty breathing, collapse or blue/grey lips require urgent assessment.

Possible stroke

Sudden facial drooping, arm weakness, difficulty speaking, confusion or sudden loss of balance: call emergency services immediately.

Severe bleeding or major injury

Seek immediate help for uncontrolled bleeding, major trauma, deep wounds or a suspected serious fracture.

Severe allergic reaction

Swelling of the tongue or throat, difficulty breathing or collapse may indicate anaphylaxis and needs immediate emergency care.

Unresponsive person or seizure emergency

If someone is unresponsive, has abnormal breathing or a prolonged/repeated seizure, call emergency services and follow dispatcher instructions.

Other rapidly worsening symptoms

Severe sudden pain, poisoning, serious burns or any rapidly deteriorating condition may need emergency assessment.

General first aid: Call your local emergency service. Follow the dispatcher's directions. Do not give food, drink or medicines to an unconscious person. If the person is not breathing normally, begin CPR/use an AED if trained or follow dispatcher guidance. Do not move someone with a possible spinal injury unless there is immediate danger.
